## Introduction

The language used for unexplained objects in the sky has changed several times since the late 1940s. “Flying saucer” gave way to “UFO” (unidentified flying object), and in recent official usage “UAP” (unidentified anomalous phenomena) has increasingly replaced both. These shifts changed how governments, journalists, researchers, and the public discussed unusual sightings. They did not, however, change the evidential standards applied to claims about extraterrestrial visitors, secret technologies, or predicted contact events.

This distinction matters in debates about failed UFO-related predictions. A prophecy that alien craft will appear on a specific date can fail regardless of whether officials call unexplained reports flying saucers, UFOs, or UAPs. The terminology has evolved; the burden of proof has not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us/nasa-panel-hold-first-public-meeting-ufo-study-ahead-report-2023-05-31/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nasa panel hold first public meeting ufo study ahead report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA UFO panel in first public meeting says better data...Jun 1, 2023 — Members of an independent NASA panel studying UFOs, or wh...</span></span></span>

## The Flying Saucer Image and Early UFO Language

The modern era of the subject is usually traced to pilot Kenneth Arnold's sighting near Mount Rainier on 24 June 1947. News reports quickly popularised the phrase “flying saucer” after Arnold described the motion of the objects he observed. The expression spread rapidly through newspapers and became the defining image of the phenomenon in the public imagination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Arnold_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kenneth Arnold UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 28, 2025 — Arnold&#x27;s description of the objects also led to the press quickly coining the terms flying saucer and flying disc as...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 28, 2025</span></span></span>

The problem with “flying saucer” was that it implied a particular shape. Investigators soon encountered reports involving spheres, lights, discs, cigar-shaped objects, and phenomena that did not resemble saucers at all. As official investigations expanded through projects such as Sign, Grudge, and Blue Book, a more neutral label became attractive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">project blue book 50th anniversary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue...Project Blue Book, from March 1952 to December 1969—the...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1952</span></span></span>

The term “UFO” emerged within the US Air Force during the early 1950s and is commonly associated with Captain Edward J. Ruppelt, who sought language that described a report's status rather than its appearance. Instead of suggesting a disc-shaped craft, “unidentified flying object” simply indicated that an object had not yet been identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">official investigations into UFOs: Project Grudge and Project Bluebook. During his time as director, Ruppelt had coined the term UFO. [ci...</span></span></span>

This linguistic change was significant because it moved the discussion from imagery to investigation. A flying saucer sounded like a thing. A UFO described a condition of [uncertainty]({{ 'uncertainty/' | relative_url }}).

### What the New Term Was Supposed to Accomplish

Official documents from the period show frustration with the popular “flying saucer” label. Investigators recognised that the phrase carried assumptions before any evidence had been examined. Some Air Force materials explicitly referred to “flying saucer” as an unfortunate term because it encouraged conclusions based on appearance and popular mythology rather than analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/061226/release_03/documents/CIA-UAP-015-Project_Blue_Book_Special_Report_No_14.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CIA UAP 015 Project Blue Book Special Report No 14</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarRETURN IMMEDIATELY AFTER USE TO THE CIA...... unidentified aerial objects have been received by the Air Force from many...</span></span></span>

The adoption of UFO therefore represented an attempt to make the subject more procedural. It did not establish that any object was alien. Instead, it created a category for reports awaiting explanation.

That distinction became important whenever dramatic predictions were attached to sightings. Claims that extraterrestrial fleets would arrive, rescue believers, reveal themselves publicly, or transform civilisation were always stronger claims than the underlying observation that something remained unidentified.

## Why UAP Became the Official Term

By the early twenty-first century, “UFO” had accumulated decades of cultural baggage. For many people the term had become almost synonymous with alien spacecraft, conspiracy theories, and science-fiction imagery. Officials increasingly argued that the language itself discouraged reporting and distorted public understanding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us/nasa-panel-hold-first-public-meeting-ufo-study-ahead-report-2023-05-31/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters+2Nevada Current]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nasa panel hold first public meeting ufo study ahead report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA UFO panel in first public meeting says better data...Jun 1, 2023 — Members of an independent NASA panel studying UFOs, or wh...</span></span></span>

As a result, government agencies and scientific bodies began adopting “UAP”. Initially this usually meant “unidentified aerial phenomena”. More recently, official US usage has expanded it to “unidentified anomalous phenomena”, broadening the category beyond objects apparently observed in the air.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us/nasa-panel-hold-first-public-meeting-ufo-study-ahead-report-2023-05-31/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters+2NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nasa panel hold first public meeting ufo study ahead report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA UFO panel in first public meeting says better data...Jun 1, 2023 — Members of an independent NASA panel studying UFOs, or wh...</span></span></span>

Several practical motives drove the change:

* **Reducing [stigma]({{ 'stigma/' | relative_url }}).** Officials argued that pilots and other observers might be more willing to report unusual events if the terminology was less associated with extraterrestrial claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/sep/14/ufo-nasa-research-chief-announcement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This initiative aims to collect and analyze data, demystify sightings, and promote a science-based perspective. The use of AI and machine...</span></span></span>
* **Emphasising data collection.** UAP language focuses attention on observations, sensors, and anomalies rather than assumed explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAPJun 9, 2022 — On September 14, 2023, the NASA Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Independent Study Team published its final...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>
* **Expanding scope.** Modern investigations may involve radar tracks, infrared signatures, satellite observations, or events reported across different domains, making “flying object” seem too narrow.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/us/nasa-panel-hold-first-public-meeting-ufo-study-ahead-report-2023-05-31/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nasa panel hold first public meeting ufo study ahead report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA UFO panel in first public meeting says better data...Jun 1, 2023 — Members of an independent NASA panel studying UFOs, or wh...</span></span></span>

The shift also reflects a broader effort to treat reports as questions for analysis rather than evidence for a particular conclusion. NASA's UAP work, for example, has repeatedly stressed the need for higher-quality data and a scientific approach rather than assumptions about origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAPJun 9, 2022 — On September 14, 2023, the NASA Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Independent Study Team published its final...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## What Terminology Cannot Prove After a Missed Prediction

A common misunderstanding in failed-prediction debates is that a change in terminology somehow changes the strength of the evidence. It does not.

If a prediction claimed that alien craft would appear on a specified date and no such event occurred, the prediction remains failed whether the expected craft were described as flying saucers, UFOs, or UAPs. The vocabulary can affect tone and perception, but it cannot rescue an incorrect forecast.

This becomes clearer when comparing what the terms actually mean:

TermWhat it describesFlying saucerA popular image or apparent shapeUFOAn object that has not yet been identifiedUAPAn anomalous observation that remains unexplainedAlien spacecraftA specific explanation requiring evidence

Only the last category makes a claim about origin. The others describe uncertainty.

Official investigations have repeatedly preserved this distinction. Project Blue Book recorded hundreds of cases that remained unidentified, yet the Air Force did not treat “unidentified” as proof of extraterrestrial technology. Likewise, modern NASA and Pentagon discussions acknowledge [unresolved]({{ 'unresolved/' | relative_url }}) cases while stating that no evidence currently demonstrates extraterrestrial origins.
